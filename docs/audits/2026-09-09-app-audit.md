# PuckIQ product and app audit — September 9, 2026

## Recommendation

Build **the personal hockey-season companion that tells you what changed before puck drop**.

The product promise: **“Your teams. Tonight’s changes. The numbers behind them.”** Follow teams and players, get a short statistical briefing, open one complete game sheet, receive material updates, then see how expectations compared with the result. The same game and its evidence should persist through that whole journey.

PuckIQ has useful raw material: team comparisons, player trends, game models, prediction storage, a consistent dark/cyan foundation, and a substantial passing test suite. Today, however, those pieces do not form a dependable product. The strongest-looking signals sometimes combine incompatible data periods; important detail links discard context; the subscription and notification journeys are disconnected. I would repair those before spending on acquisition or adding another feature family.

This is a product recommendation, not evidence of willingness to pay. It follows the existing owner-approved “stats-focused hockey enthusiast” direction. Fantasy managers may have stronger explicit willingness to pay, but the current fantasy implementation would require a larger coherent rebuild. No new positioning, prices, production code, remote data, or store listing was changed by this audit.

## What a paying user should experience

### A repeatable day

| Moment | Experience | Reason to return |
|---|---|---|
| Morning, about a minute | Three relevant changes for followed teams/players; yesterday’s result against the pregame expectation; next scheduled game. | Catch up without assembling several stat pages. |
| Before puck drop | A complete game sheet: probability, three leading factors, recent versus season rates, rest, confirmed/projected/unknown goalie state, source and prediction times. | Understand the matchup and what remains uncertain. |
| Material change | An opt-in alert opens that exact game/player and highlights what changed since the last view. | Avoid repeated checking; see a change with context. |
| After the game | Final result beside the frozen pregame forecast, relevant box-score differences, followed-player updates. | Close the loop and learn which signals held up. |
| Across the season | A history of followed teams/players and saved matchups, weekly trend comparisons, standings context. | The app becomes more useful as the user’s season accumulates. |

The key feature is **change history attached to a useful game sheet**. Scores, percentages and favorites already exist elsewhere. The paid hypothesis is that PuckIQ can assemble the relevant evidence faster, present it more clearly, and reliably bring the user back when something meaningful changes.

Do not imply that a goalie confirmation necessarily changes the forecast. Recompute only when the model actually uses the new input; preserve both snapshots, identify the model version, and show the resulting change. If the model is unchanged, say so. Do not describe a correlation or a heuristic as a causal explanation.

### First use

1. Choose a favorite team and optionally a few players. Browse without creating an account.
2. Show that team’s real next game, or a clearly dated latest-season view when there is no scheduled game.
3. Let the user open the game sheet and inspect the evidence immediately.
4. Offer “Follow this game” and explain the specific notification being enabled. Ask for system notification permission at that moment.
5. Offer paid access after demonstrating a real useful change/history view. Account creation, purchase and restore must preserve the selections already made.

Target: a new fan can reach a useful, correctly sourced game or team insight in under 60 seconds. This is an acceptance target, not a measured current result.

### Navigation and scope

Use **Today / Following / Players / League** as the proposed four primary destinations. Put account/settings in a header control. Put team comparison inside League and link it directly from a matchup. Keep advanced model editing accessible inside analysis tools after its scoring/backtest behavior is honest.

- **Keep and improve:** game data, standings, team/player detail, recent-form comparisons, clear model explanations, prediction history, native alerts, and the existing Stat Sheet visual identity.
- **Build first:** a real game detail destination; a single follow model shared by Today and alerts; source freshness and season metadata; persisted before/after/result history.
- **Defer:** trade calculators, fantasy-platform imports, waiver automation, public leaderboards, achievements, new model families, social feeds, and more dashboard customization. Each creates another product to maintain before the main loop works.
- **Fantasy choice:** if the owner chooses fantasy as the primary customer, restore My Team as a primary destination, persist the roster, support the actual league scoring/roster rules, and prove recommendation delivery. Do not leave a fantasy sales pitch attached to a fan-focused navigation.
- **Betting choice:** requires market prices, timestamped prediction/odds comparisons and independent performance evidence. This audit establishes no betting advantage. Win probability alone is not an estimate of wagering value.

### Three plausible directions

| Direction | Strength | Tradeoff | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal statistical season companion | Fits the existing owner vision and much of the app’s data/UI. | A paid habit must outperform free tools on convenience and trustworthy context. | **Build and validate this first.** |
| Fantasy decision assistant | A clear recurring decision and existing willingness to spend in the category. | Roster, rules, data latency and recommendation contracts need substantial work; platform incumbents own the roster. | Choose only as an explicit product pivot. |
| Prediction challenge | Closer to the current public store promise; offers a season-long participation loop. | Paying for participation is unproven; needs honest scoring/history rather than confidence hype. | Possible later retention layer after the model ledger is correct. |

### Proposed offer to test

**Free:** dependable scores and standings, basic player/team browsing, a favorite team, an actual game-sheet preview, and enough source/accuracy information to assess the product. Trust information and honest missing-data states should never be locked away.

**Season Pass:** the complete personal brief across followed teams/players, material-change alerts, deeper comparison windows, saved game/player history and weekly season review. Public model accountability stays visible; paid history concerns the user’s personal workspace.

Test **$19.99 versus $29.99 for a clearly defined season**, with the end date and postseason inclusion explicit. These are proposed test prices, not validated willingness to pay. Do not change production offers until the product delivers and prior-purchaser treatment is decided. Use actual store product prices and eligibility; never hardcode a trial every user may not receive.

## Design vision

Keep [Stat Sheet](../design/vision.md). The concept does not replace that design law.

- One dark card surface, one cyan decorative accent, green/red/amber only for measured direction or status.
- Real team/player identity; compact rows for peers; reserve a large hero for the single most relevant matchup or change.
- Every important number needs its quantity, period and comparison. Prefer “L5: 3.4 shots/game · season: 2.6” to a floating HOT badge.
- Short explanatory labels are useful. Articles, pep talks, invented certainties and model jargon are not.
- A user should be able to inspect the evidence from the card rather than hunting in Compare.
- A PRO marker can explain deeper access after useful content is visible. Do not make an empty locked screen the default product demonstration.

### Validation before expanding scope

Recruit roughly 15–25 serious hockey fans for a two-week game-day pilot, including existing purchasers. This is a discovery sample, not a statistically powered pricing experiment. Observe real sessions and record why they returned or chose an alternative.

Proposed decision gates, to agree before launch:

- At least 70% reach a useful followed-team/game insight within 60 seconds without help.
- At least half of activated pilot users return on three distinct relevant game days in week two.
- At least five pilot users actually pay the proposed season price after experiencing the product. Praise and stated purchase intent do not count as transactions.
- Every delivered alert opens the correct entity; measure duplicates, incorrect status and source-to-delivery delay. Establish the latency target from the actual provider before advertising “instant.”
- Ask which external app/site this replaces for the task. If users still perform the same research elsewhere, identify the missing trust or convenience rather than adding features blindly.

Measure `follow_saved`, `brief_opened`, `game_sheet_opened`, `evidence_opened`, `alert_opt_in`, `alert_opened`, `result_reviewed`, `paywall_viewed`, `purchase_completed` and `restore_completed`, with season, entity, model/source version and freshness where appropriate. Report cohort denominators. Define the main repeat-use metric as **weekly fans who inspect a followed game or player on at least three distinct relevant days**, with “relevant days” tied to the entities they follow.

Check costs before setting price: reliable goalie/injury/line sources, ingestion, forecast compute, notification operations, store fees and support. A season pass is attractive only if those costs support it. No cost or revenue forecast was established in this audit.
